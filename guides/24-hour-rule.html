The 24-hour rule: when to wait before you buy

Most regret comes from buying fast. A waiting rule gives the excitement time to fade, so you can see whether you still want the thing. The trick is matching the wait to the price, and the clearest way to measure the price is in hours of work.

Match the wait to the work

It costs youWhat to do
Under 1 hourBuy it if you will use it.
Under 1 workdayAsk: would I still want it tomorrow?
1 to 5 workdaysWait 24 hours.
Over 5 workdaysWait 7 days and compare prices.

These are the same levels the HoursToPay calculator uses for its verdicts.

How to do the wait

  1. Don't check out. Leave it in the cart, or add it to your wishlist in the calculator.
  2. Write down why you want it. One sentence is enough.
  3. Come back after the wait. Read your sentence. If the reason still holds, buy it without guilt.

Five questions to ask when the wait is over

  • Would I spend those hours working for this, if I had to work them today?
  • Do I already own something that does the same job?
  • Where will it live, and how often will I use it in the next month?
  • Is this the best price, or is the "sale" just urgency?
  • Are my bills and savings covered first?

When the rule doesn't apply

Needs are not impulse buys. Medicine, a broken phone you work on, or a repair that stops a bigger cost later should not wait for a rule. The rule is for wants that feel urgent.

Flash sales and countdown timers

Countdown clocks exist to make you skip the wait. If a deal disappears before your 24 hours are up, you have still saved the full price. Sales come back far more often than they claim.
